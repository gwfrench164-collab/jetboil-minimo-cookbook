## Official Recipe

Source: [Bobsadilla - The Breakfast Quesadilla](https://jetboil.johnsonoutdoors.com/us/recipes/bobsadilla-breakfast-quesadilla)

Published wording from the recorded source extract; only markup and outer whitespace are normalized.

### Ingredients

- 2 Tbsp of diced bell pepper
- 2 Tbsp of diced tomatoes
- 1 to 2 Tbsp of diced onions
- 1 Tbsp of olive oil
- 1 Tsp of butter
- 2 Eggs
- 1 Tortilla
- 1 to 2 oz Mozzarella or cheddar cheese, or whatever you prefer

### Instructions

1. In a Jetboil Summit Skillet, add the olive oil. Once heated, add the vegetables and sauté for approximately 2 to 3 minutes. Set them aside.

2. Butter a pan and allow to heat. Once heated, break two eggs in the pan. Immediately put a tortilla over the eggs and apply pressure to the tortilla so you smoosh the eggs underneath a bit. Let the concoction cook for a couple minutes then gently work a spatula between the fried egg/tortilla and the pan.

3. Flip the tortilla over. Add shredded cheddar cheese (or any type you like really) and the sautéed veggies. Fold the tortilla in half and let it sit over low heat until the cheese melts a bit.

4. Or even better, if you have a cover for your pan, throw a splash of water in the pan and cover the pan with the lid while the water evaporates. It will steam the whole Bobsadilla and the cheese will melt nicely without burning the outside of the tortilla.

## MiniMo Adaptation

Editorial notes; not official recipe text and not field-tested.

This is a skillet recipe. For the MiniMo, use the Pot Support and compatible skillet; the official French Toast recipe documents the MiniMo, Pot Support and Summit Skillet combination.

The official method uses one tortilla pressed onto eggs, then flipped and folded. A two-tortilla scrambled-egg sandwich is a proposed variation, not a transcription.

Use the low-heat stage in the published method; no field-tested burner setting or fuel consumption has been established.

Equipment sequence: the MiniMo manual requires ignition with no pot or skillet on the burner, followed by careful placement of cookware. Follow that manual sequence wherever the recipe wording is abbreviated or ambiguous.
